# English text for the navigation illustrations

Edited text accompanying the supplied YUE-FAN diagrams. The original images retain their embedded labels; the portfolio provides these revised descriptions as readable text.

## Upstream towing

**Tracking beams**

Each beam secures three bamboo towing lines.

**Bow upright**

A removable hardwood post, extending 2.5 m above the deck, supports the lifting line.

**Lifting line and iron ring**

Pulling the line taut raises the ring so that the towing line clears the bow. The lifting line attaches to a retaining pin at the post’s base.

**Shore line and handspike**

The line is secured to a rock ashore; the handspike tightens and holds it. The supplied annotation identifies its material as uncertain.

**Forward compartments**

The foremost compartment has no decking; the second remains undecked while underway. The indicated deck plank is removed for this arrangement.

**Grass-line pennants**

Used with a boat hook to help check the boat’s movement.

## Downstream steering

**Laodah / 老大**

The boatmaster, shown operating the stern sweep.

**Flying bridge**

An elevated platform for operating the long stern sweep. Bracing ropes steady the platform and limit sideways movement.

**Raised stern**

Supports the sweep and keeps the two stern sweeps from interfering with each other.

**T’ai-kung**

Works at the bow, operates the bow sweep, and checks water depth with a boat hook.

**Stern, side, and bow sweeps**

Used for steering.

**Oars**

Used for propulsion.

Sources: Worcester (1941), plates 10 and 18; Audemard (1963), volume 5, plate 30. The phrase “the old great one” in the supplied photograph is rendered here as “boatmaster.” The downstream heading emphasizes steering, which describes most of the labelled equipment.
