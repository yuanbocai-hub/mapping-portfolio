Zhiting Cai · University of Washington, Seattle

Mapping Portfolio

I study geography and GIS at the University of Washington, with a minor in Aquatic and Fishery Sciences. My research follows the rivers of monsoonal Asia. On the West River, I study how the timing and duration of floods relate to fish fry production. I also use historical maps and travel accounts to trace freight routes on the upper Yangtze and trade routes across mountain passes in the Brahmaputra basin.

01 / GIS in Himalayan Regional Studies

Corridors before Borders

A study of the cartographic legacy of the Survey of India and the Royal Geographical Society in the eastern Himalaya.

Conference Project · ACPSS 2026

Koloriang and Leporiang lie about 75 km apart in different river basins. Both names contain the Nyishi element -riang, meaning “land.” I recorded each appearance of a place name on a historical map separately, keeping variant spellings and uncertain matches in a gazetteer of 1,582 records.

02 / GEOG 458 · Course assignment

Following the Tsangpo

Following F. M. Bailey, a British political officer serving in Assam, in search of the elusive falls of the Tsangpo–Brahmaputra in 1913.

Beyond the reach Bailey could see, his account drew on what Monba hunters told him. The story map leaves that stretch of his route open.

03 / Historical Geography

The Yangtze as a river transportation system

At Yichang, crews shifted cargo from middle-river boats to junks built for the rapids upstream. My reconstruction of a 1935 commodity map puts that port in a wider landscape, from the lakes and plains of the middle Yangtze to the tributaries of Sichuan.

Part of my commercial model-building project on the crooked-stern junk.

Following G. R. G. Worcester’s accounts to reconstruct the Yangtze’s shipping networks in the late Qing and Republican periods.

About this collection

I use maps to examine how rivers connect places, how people make journeys through difficult terrain, and how those relationships enter the historical record. These projects bring source comparison and spatial analysis into different forms of presentation, from a regional commodity map to an interactive account of an expedition.
