West River / 西江 · A photographic field project

Encountering the
Many Embodiments
of the West River(Xi Fleuve)

同一条江河，不同的人间

“All rivers have distinct personalities, intangible traits that go beyond width and length and swiftness.”

— Peter Hessler, River Town

Peter Hessler describes the contrasting personalities of the Yangtze and the Wu through their landscapes, journeys, and human presence. His observation leaves me wondering how many personalities might inhabit a single river. Walking along the West River, I have encountered boat crews, fishers, engineers, and riverside residents whose work, knowledge, and attachments bring them into different relationships with the same waters.

Why “Embodiments”?Encountering Water through Mahayana Buddhist Thought

The title draws on the understanding of the Buddha’s three bodies, or trikāya, in Mahayana Buddhist thought: dharmakāya (法身), saṃbhogakāya (報身), and nirmāṇakāya (化身). These terms concern the nature of buddhahood and the forms through which awakened presence becomes accessible to different beings. The Sūtra on the Three Bodies expresses their relationship through an atmospheric analogy: space, clouds, and rain. Its imagery offers a point of departure for this photographic project’s attention to water, appearance, and encounter.

I approach this imagery as a way of thinking about the relationship between existence, representation, and situated experience. The analogy is a personal extension of the teaching, rather than a correspondence between the three bodies and water’s physical states. Water circulates through rivers, clouds, rainfall, and human bodies; we encounter it in particular places, through particular needs and histories. A river, a cup of water, and a passing cloud make that circulation perceptible in different ways.

Along the West River, these encounters acquire social and material form. Water becomes a navigable channel through the knowledge and labour of boat crews, a fishing ground through the practices of fishers, and an energy resource through engineering measurements and planning. These relationships overlap within the same waters, while the people and institutions involved have unequal capacities to shape the river’s future. “Embodiments” names my attempt to photograph how the river becomes present in these lives—and how each encounter reveals something while leaving other relationships beyond the frame.

Textual reference: The Sūtra on the Three Bodies, Toh 283, translated by the Buddhavacana Translation Group for 84000.

Hydrology · R

Summer flows at Dahuangjiangkou

Datengxia began storing water in 2020. To put two later summers in perspective, I compared daily discharge at Dahuangjiangkou in 2022 and 2024 with a seasonal curve fitted to Dongta gauge records from 1936–1941 using a generalized additive model (GAM).

The two gauges and observation periods differ. Attributing a departure to dam operations would require evidence beyond this comparison. The first two date windows overlap.

Datengxia’s river closure took place in October 2019 and initial impoundment in March 2020. National Energy Administration.
